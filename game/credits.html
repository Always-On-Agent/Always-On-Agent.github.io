ALWAYS-ON / OPEN PROTOTYPE

Two worlds. One continuing loop.

Explore an open village with a borrowed book, or visit five posters and Booth 44 in an ECCV-inspired exhibition hall. Both experiences connect visible evidence, retained context, and user choices through a pair of glasses.

Scenario and glasses interface

The Always-On Agent team authored the two world layouts, scripted interactions, bilingual glasses interfaces, and website integration. This prototype runs a single-player server inside the browser. There is no account, remote game server, camera, microphone, or live AI model. Scene observations and assistant decisions are scripted.

Preferences and the event journal stay in browser storage. Each reload starts a fresh book-return episode while retaining prior context; old notice information remains unverified until observed again. The return box removes the tagged book from the player's inventory and records a receipt in the current session. It does not use a persistent chest inventory.

ECCV · EgoPoster

The conference experience draws on EgoPoster and its documented five-poster demonstration. It follows the workflow of noticing a poster, accumulating a research card, carrying questions to another poster, and reviewing the visit at Action Intelligence’s Booth 44.

The hall is an original, condensed reconstruction: white modular poster boards, a dark exhibition floor, overhead trusses, and a compact booth. Its scale, furniture, abstract poster diagrams, and walking route are adapted for browser interaction. It is not an exact replica of the venue.

Poster identity is admitted only after an unobstructed, sustained view. Requested detail inspection requires a changed viewpoint at close range. Cards, explicit preferences, and feedback remain in this browser; earlier observations are marked as historical until revisited. The report includes this visit’s observed posters and recorded interactions, and is downloaded only when the visitor confirms. No email is sent.

Research summaries, question prompts, and recommendations are authored scenario content. Looking at the simplified boards triggers this content; the demo does not recognize text, transcribe conversations, run a live model, or demonstrate policy learning.
