01 / THE PERSPECTIVE

Assistance continues.
The request does not have to.

Always-on personal AI sustains assistance between explicit requests, under a declared schedule and availability contract, while retaining usable user-specific state across interactions.

It carries unresolved commitments forward and chooses when to observe, remember, communicate, prepare, act—or remain silent. Self-evolution extends this baseline: attributed outcomes must justify policy changes that are validated and retained.

02 / THE FRAMEWORK

Three modules. Five connections.
One continuing loop.

We organize the field around what a personal assistant senses, retains, and does—and how these decisions depend on one another.

Follow the connections.

Explore a module, or select a directed channel to follow the dependency it carries.

ModerateHigh difficulty

Five channels beyond sense-and-respond. S → A is the basic observation-to-action path. We focus on the other five directed dependencies.

How to read the difficulty ratings

Moderate and High express our qualitative assessment under changing user needs, limited resources, and incomplete feedback. They describe challenges in continuing operation, not a universal ordering or a ranking of research maturity.

Framework definitions in the survey ↗
Functional module / S

Sensing

Acquire and interpret current evidence. Decide what to observe, when to sample, and what uncertainty remains.

01
Observation streams

Work with egocentric video, streaming audio, physical sensors, and digital activity. Preserve when and where evidence was observed.

02
Sampling and interpretation

Choose observation windows, attention, event boundaries, and compression. Keep useful context while accounting for gaps, ambiguity, and sensitive information.

03
Continuing operation

Balance event coverage and timely interpretation against energy, latency, and access. Observation can be scheduled or triggered; always-on assistance need not record continuously.

A continuing reminder

A reminder is pending. Observe whether a call has ended, while keeping uncertainty explicit when audio or calendar evidence is incomplete.

A short-lived frame buffer supports current perception. Cross-interaction personal state belongs to Memory.

Functional module / M

Memory

Keep personal information usable across interactions. Admit, organize, retrieve, consolidate, correct, and forget as circumstances change.

01
What persists

Retain facts and preferences, resumable tasks and commitments, and useful cases or skills. Preserve identity, time, scope, and provenance.

02
Admission and retrieval

Decide what deserves a durable record, organize it, and retrieve evidence relevant to the current decision within the available budget.

03
Correction and forgetting

Resolve conflicts, consolidate accumulated experience, and retire stale information. Carry corrections and deletions through summaries and other derived records.

A continuing reminder

Keep both the pending task and the user's quiet-morning preference, including their source and scope, so a later interaction can resume appropriately.

Having a record is only the first step. Its relevance, validity, and availability to later decisions determine whether it remains useful.

Read the Memory chapter ↗
Functional module / A

Action

Decide whether, when, and how to intervene. Communicate, clarify, prepare, execute, wait, or remain silent within the user's current authority.

01
Intervention and timing

Weigh the value of assistance against interruption and uncertainty. Choose an appropriate moment, including a deliberate decision to wait or stay silent.

02
Control and continuity

Take a single step, plan, delegate, or resume interrupted work. Carry unfinished commitments forward across interactions.

03
Execution and authority

Choose a conversational, GUI, API, or physical interface. Check current permission at commitment and execution, and distinguish issuing an operation from achieving its effect.

A continuing reminder

Defer a routine reminder during a call, retain the pending commitment, and reconsider when an appropriate opportunity arrives.

Remembered preferences help personalize a decision; they do not create new permission to act.

S → M / Persistent admission

From observations to lasting context

Select sensed evidence for persistent admission. The channel requests creation or refresh of a record; Memory decides how to realize the write. Admission may be user-directed or autonomous.

Why it matters for always-on

What survives today's interaction determines what tomorrow's assistance can recover. The system must preserve useful context without retaining everything it encounters.

Why it is difficult
Moderate

Difficulty under continuing operation

  • Future usefulness is uncertain when an observation arrives; a missed event may never be recoverable.
  • Excess retention consumes resources and increases privacy exposure; compression can lose the context needed later.
A continuing reminder

Retain a newly stated task and quiet-morning preference, with their source, time, and scope, so both remain available after the conversation ends.

Boundary. A transient observation buffer or a preloaded profile alone does not establish online, persistent admission.

M → A / State-informed action

From retained context to better decisions

Use retained personal state to condition whether, when, and how the assistant acts, including its content, interface, and level of commitment, within current grants of authority.

Why it matters for always-on

Earlier interactions should influence later assistance when they are relevant. The assistant must reconcile remembered context with the user's present circumstances.

Why it is difficult
Moderate

Difficulty under continuing operation

  • Personal records can become stale or conflict with one another and with current evidence.
  • A valid memory may still be irrelevant to this decision; its use must fit timing, context, and current permission.
A continuing reminder

Use the quiet-morning preference to defer a routine reminder, while checking whether an approaching deadline changes the appropriate timing.

Boundary. Retrieval alone does not show that a memory changed the action, improved it, or authorized it.

A → S / Action-directed observation

From an action to the evidence it needs

Turn action intentions, plans, and execution state into observation targets. Acquire information needed before acting and check expected effects during or after execution.

Why it matters for always-on

Plans may outlive an interaction while the world keeps changing. Observation needs must follow the active commitment, including when work is interrupted or resumed.

Why it is difficult
Moderate

Difficulty under continuing operation

  • The system must translate uncertainty about an action into a specific, timely observation.
  • Partial visibility and changing plans complicate verification: checking too early, too late, or against an obsolete expectation can mislead.
A continuing reminder

A planned reminder prompts a check that the call has ended. After a requested calendar edit, inspect the resulting event to confirm the intended change.

Boundary. An external evaluator checking success does not establish an observation-and-verification mechanism inside the service.

M → S / Memory-guided observation

From prior knowledge to selective attention

Use retained personal state to guide new sampling, attention, compression, or interpretation. Personal priors can change what is observed and how the same evidence is understood.

Why it matters for always-on

Remembered routines can make ongoing observation more selective. As routines change, the service must preserve opportunities to acquire evidence that corrects its own assumptions.

Why it is difficult
High

Difficulty under continuing operation

  • A stale prior can suppress the very observations needed to discover that the prior is wrong.
  • Keeping corrective evidence available competes with sensing budgets, privacy constraints, and the cost of asking the user.
A continuing reminder

A remembered routine guides when to check availability for a reminder. Leave opportunities to notice an unexpected schedule change instead of always skipping that period.

Boundary. Retrieving memory only to compose an answer is M → A. M → S requires an effect on observation or interpretation.

A → M / Outcome-attributed revision

From outcomes to justified updates

Link externally observed consequences, including those of silence, to earlier decisions. Use that attribution to govern persistent changes to personal state or decision policies.

Why it matters for always-on

Feedback can arrive well after an intervention and affect many future interactions. Persistent updates need evidence that supports both the proposed change and its scope.

Why it is difficult
High

Difficulty under continuing operation

  • Sparse, delayed outcomes mix several possible causes, including other actions and changes in the user's circumstances.
  • Evidence that supports correcting one task record may not justify changing a general intervention policy.
A continuing reminder

After a reminder is dismissed, determine whether timing, obsolete content, or task completion explains the response before deciding what should change persistently.

Boundary. Logging the dismissal is S → M. Linking it to a decision and judging which lasting revision it supports is A → M.

03 / LEVELS OF LOOP CLOSURE

From remembering
to learning responsibly.

Five cumulative levels extend a session-local L0 baseline. Continuing operation arrives at L3; self-evolution requires the additional commitments of L5.

  1. L1

    Retain personal state

    Keep usable user-specific state across interactions.

    PERSISTENCE
  2. L2

    Use what is retained

    Let retained state inform a later Sensing or Action decision.

    REUSE
  3. L3

    Sustain a continuing loop

    Create scheduled or event-triggered decision opportunities between requests, including waiting and silence.

    ALWAYS-ON BASELINE
  4. L4

    Close the outcome-feedback path

    Link external outcome evidence to earlier decisions and use it to check or correct assistance.

    FEEDBACK
  5. L5

    Govern persistent policy revision

    Use attributed outcomes to propose, validate, and durably commit changes to future behavior.

    SELF-EVOLUTION

A level describes supported closure requirements. It does not, by itself, establish service quality or lasting user benefit.

04 / FROM ARCHITECTURE TO EVIDENCE

Meet HALO.

Harness for Always-on Loop Oversight

31configurations reviewed
across 30 works

A working connection is a mechanism claim. A useful continuing service needs evidence about its effects. HALO keeps those judgments distinct and traceable.

READ MODE

Review what the sources establish.

Inspect papers and available artifacts. Link channel judgments to source locations, evaluated configurations, and the comparisons reported by the original study.

Used for the review in this survey
RUN MODE

Test a scoped configuration.

Connect an executable system through a suitable interface or adapter. Record manipulation checks, traces, and measured endpoints under specified conditions.

Protocol for scoped execution

Four forms of supporting evidence

Mechanism descriptionTraced useControlled effectControlled service outcome

These forms are not cumulative. Each claim retains its configuration, comparison, setting, horizon, and uncertainty.
