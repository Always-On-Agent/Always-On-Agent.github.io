01 / THE PERSPECTIVE

Assistance continues.
The request does not have to.

Always-on personal AI sustains assistance between explicit requests, under a declared schedule and availability contract, while retaining usable user-specific state across interactions.

It carries unresolved commitments forward and chooses when to observe, remember, communicate, prepare, act—or remain silent. Self-evolution extends this baseline: attributed outcomes must justify policy changes that are validated and retained.

02 / THE FRAMEWORK

Three modules. Five connections.
One continuing loop.

We organize the field around what a personal assistant senses, retains, and does—and how these decisions depend on one another.

S

Sensing

Acquire and interpret timely evidence. Decide what to observe, when to sample, and what uncertainty remains.

M

Memory

Maintain usable personal state. Admit, consolidate, retrieve, correct, and forget information across interactions.

A

Action

Choose the right intervention. Communicate, prepare, execute, wait, or stay silent within the user’s authority.

Follow the connections

Select a channel to see the dependency it carries.

From observations to lasting context

Which observations become persistent personal state? Admission and compression choices shape what later interactions can recover.

Example: retain a newly stated preference, with its source and scope.

From retained context to better decisions

Which memories reach the action policy, and how do they affect timing, content, preparation, execution, or silence?

Example: use a remembered meeting commitment to time a reminder.

From an action to the evidence it needs

Actions create observation needs: acquire relevant information before acting and check uncertain effects afterward.

Example: inspect the current workspace before editing, then verify the result.

From prior knowledge to selective attention

Memory can guide sampling, attention, and interpretation. The challenge is to preserve opportunities to discover that a prior is wrong.

Example: monitor a familiar routine while still detecting an unexpected change.

From outcomes to justified updates

Link outcomes to earlier decisions, revise personal state, and assess whether evidence warrants a persistent policy change.

Example: update a reminder preference after an explicit user correction.
SHARED CONDITIONS

Privacy · Resources · Uncertainty · User authority

03 / LEVELS OF LOOP CLOSURE

From remembering
to learning responsibly.

Five cumulative levels extend a session-local L0 baseline. Continuing operation arrives at L3; self-evolution requires the additional commitments of L5.

  1. L1

    Retain personal state

    Keep usable user-specific state across interactions.

    PERSISTENCE
  2. L2

    Use what is retained

    Let retained state inform a later Sensing or Action decision.

    REUSE
  3. L3

    Sustain a continuing loop

    Create scheduled or event-triggered decision opportunities between requests, including waiting and silence.

    ALWAYS-ON BASELINE
  4. L4

    Close the outcome-feedback path

    Link external outcome evidence to earlier decisions and use it to check or correct assistance.

    FEEDBACK
  5. L5

    Govern persistent policy revision

    Use attributed outcomes to propose, validate, and durably commit changes to future behavior.

    SELF-EVOLUTION

A level describes supported closure requirements. It does not, by itself, establish service quality or lasting user benefit.

05 / APPLICATIONS

A shared loop.
Different worlds to operate in.

The observation domain, people involved, and outcome horizon change what reliable assistance requires.

01

Situated daily assistance

Connect current egocentric context to timely help and correctable personal histories.

02

Conversation & meetings

Balance turn timing, interruption, audience boundaries, and commitments across sessions.

03

Digital workspaces

Carry plans and procedures across tools while keeping remembered context and authority current.

04

Physical collaboration

Coordinate with people and verify world changes when outcomes are uncertain or hard to reverse.

TWO PRIORITIES AHEAD

Keep corrective evidence available.

Memory-guided observation should still let the assistant notice when routines, preferences, and circumstances change.

Make persistent changes earn their place.

Connect attributed outcomes to acceptance checks and later user benefit before committing a policy revision.
